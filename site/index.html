First time? Here's what to click.

On a Mac

  1. Double-click SETUP. When macOS says it can't verify it, click Done.
  2. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.
  3. If it asks whether Terminal may use files on a removable volume, click Allow.

On Windows

  1. Right-click the ZIP, choose Extract All, then double-click SETUP-Windows in the new folder.
  2. If Windows says it protected your PC, click More info, then Run anyway.

On Linux, run bash SETUP.command in a terminal. It needs python3 and curl.

What's inside

Double-click START-HERE and your browser opens to four tabs.

TAB 01

Ask

Ask in plain words, like you would online. The answer comes from the library, with numbered sources you can open. Works even with the AI off.

TAB 02

AI chat

A chat AI that runs on your laptop instead of the cloud. Show it a photo of a broken part or a label and ask about it.

TAB 03

Library

All of Wikipedia with pictures, the WikiMed medical encyclopedia, Army field and medical manuals, iFixit repair guides and FEMA's emergency guides. All searchable.

TAB 04

Map

Search an address, get driving or walking directions, or find the nearest hospital or pharmacy.

01 · PICK A PLACE

Your laptop's own disk, or an external SSD you can grab and take with you. SETUP helps you choose.

02 · FILL IT

Unzip the kit and double-click SETUP, or SETUP-Windows on Windows. It moves the kit where you chose, downloads the essentials first, then the rest over a few hours, and checks every file.

03 · TEST IT

Turn Wi-Fi off and double-click START-HERE. Everything should still answer.

04 · UPDATE IT

Whenever you like, double-click SETUP again while you're online. New models, books and maps arrive, and only what changed downloads.

Build your ownThe principles, for any AI to build from.
Help me build my own offline kit for an internet outage, on my laptop or an external SSD. Principles:

- Everything works with zero internet: local AI models, an offline library and offline maps, served on localhost and opened in a browser.
- One config file lists every source. One command brings everything up to date whenever I want. It resumes interrupted downloads, verifies checksums, and keeps the old version until the new one checks out.
- AI: a few open-weight models sized to my computer's memory (one capable default, one small and fast), run with llama.cpp's server.
- Library: Kiwix ZIM files (Wikipedia, medical references, iFixit, preparedness guides) served by kiwix-serve with full-text search.
- The AI answers from the library: it searches it, reads the top passages and cites them. It still works with the AI off.
- Maps: vector tiles for my region plus a low-detail world map, offline address search and offline directions. The AI can use them as tools.
- An offline self-test, and a one-page printed guide kept with the drive.
- On a drive, use exFAT so any computer can read it, and avoid symlinks.

First ask me about my computer, where the kit should live, how much space I have, my region and priorities. Then propose a plan before writing any code.

Questions

Before you set it up.

What is Tinfoil Kit?

Tinfoil Kit is a free, open-source kit for when the internet goes down: an AI that runs on your laptop, all of Wikipedia, medical and repair guides, and maps with directions, kept on the laptop itself or on an external drive. You set it up while you're online. After that, double-click to start it, and it all works with no internet.

Is the AI as good as ChatGPT?

Not quite. It's an open model small enough to run on a laptop, so it's a step behind the biggest cloud AIs. It's good at explaining and summarizing, the Ask tab answers from the library with sources, and none of it needs the internet.

Do I need the AI?

No. The library and the map work without it, and with the AI off, the Ask tab still searches every book and shows the best passages, with links to the full pages. To leave it out entirely, pick No AI in the kit builder: the same books and maps, with no models to download.

What do I need?

A Mac with an M chip (M1 or newer), or a Windows or Linux PC with an Intel or AMD chip. The kit goes on your laptop's own disk or on an external SSD, and a drive formatted exFAT starts on all three. 32 GB of memory runs the full-size AI; with less, you get a lighter one. The recommended kit is about 300 GB, and the kit builder above shows the total for your picks. Short on room? SETUP offers just the essentials, about 30 to 50 GB: the AI, the map, and the medical, preparedness and repair books. On your laptop's own disk, the kit always leaves 50 GB free.

Which AI will my laptop run?

It depends on how much memory it has. The kit picks for you when it starts, and you can switch models on the Ask tab.

MemoryThe AI it runs
8 GBGemma 4 E4B, the small model (5 GB). Close other apps before you ask it something.
16 to 24 GBGemma 4 12B, the mid-size model (7 GB). It reads photos too.
32 GB or moreInkling 2 Mini, the default (18 GB). Qwen3.8 27B and Gemma 4 26B fit too, for a second opinion.

SETUP says which one your laptop runs, and asks before downloading the models too big for it: skipping them saves about 50 GB on a 16 or 24 GB laptop. To see your memory, open About This Mac, or Settings > System > About on Windows. On a Windows or Linux PC the AI runs on the graphics card, so without a good one, use Gemma 4 E4B.

Does anything leave my laptop?

No. Once the kit is filled, every part runs on your laptop at 127.0.0.1 with no account and no telemetry. The only time it goes online is when you run SETUP or ./kit sync to download updates, and then it only talks to the places it downloads from: GitHub, Kiwix, Hugging Face, Protomaps, Geofabrik, GraphHopper, Adoptium, Maven Central and npm, plus nuget.org for Python on Windows and anything you add to kit.json.

What does setup change on my computer?

Very little. The models, books, maps and programs all go in one folder, TinfoilKit, in your home folder or on the drive, and nothing runs unless you start it. Besides that:

  • Windows: SETUP keeps python.org's Python (about 15 MB) in your user folder, since the kit's tools run on Python and Windows doesn't include it.
  • Mac: macOS asks you once to allow SETUP, since it's a script from the internet rather than an App Store app. On a Mac without Python, SETUP keeps one for the kit's tools (about 25 MB) in your Library folder.
  • Mac: the kit lifts macOS's "downloaded from the internet" mark from its own files, and signs a downloaded program on your Mac if it won't start otherwise, so everything opens with a double-click in an outage.
  • Mac: a kit in your home folder stays out of Time Machine backups, and a kit drive stays out of Spotlight.
  • Linux: if python3 or curl is missing, SETUP says how to install them.

Two more happen only if you ask: a home server can start the kit with the computer (./kit autostart on) and share it on your home network behind an access code. UNINSTALL deletes the folder and undoes the rest, including the Python SETUP got.

How do I know it's safe?

Every line is open to read, and the kit is built so you don't have to trust a server. It installs updates to its own code only if they're signed with the maintainer's key, which never touches GitHub. Every model, book, map and program comes straight from its publisher, checked against the publisher's checksum wherever one is posted. Its servers answer only your own computer unless you turn on sharing, which asks for a code. The README's Security section says what it protects against, what it can't, and how to report a problem.

What's the catch?

None. It's open source under the MIT license, with no account, no telemetry in the kit and no analytics on this site. It's a side project from the team behind Do They Support It. What it costs you is disk space and a few hours of downloading.

Can I trust the AI's answers?

Check it, the way you'd check a well-read friend. In the Ask tab it answers from the library and numbers its sources, so you can open the page it used. For anything medical, the kit is a place to look things up, in WikiMed and the Army's medical manuals. It isn't a doctor: read the source, and get trained help when you can.

What's under the hood?

Open-source parts, all served on your laptop at 127.0.0.1: llama.cpp's llama-server runs the AI, kiwix-serve runs the library, and Protomaps, Photon and GraphHopper run the map. One file, kit.json, lists every source. ./kit sync checks each download against its publisher's checksum and keeps the old version until the new one verifies. The README has the commands.

Can my family use it from their phones?

Yes. Sharing stays off until you turn it on: run ./kit share on (.\kit.cmd share on on Windows), or start the kit with --share for one session. Phones and laptops on your Wi-Fi or hotspot then open the address it shows and type its 8-digit access code, or scan its QR code. No internet is needed, only a local network, and other devices and websites can't use it without the code. For a computer that stays on, bash SETUP.command --home-server (SETUP-Windows.bat --home-server on Windows) sets it up to share and to start with the computer.

How long does it run on battery?

The big model uses a lot of power: expect a couple of hours of active use on a laptop. The small model uses much less, and the Library tab with the AI off uses almost nothing. A USB-C power station and a folding solar panel keep it going.

Does it run on Windows or Linux?

Yes, all of it: the AI, the library and the map. On Windows, double-click SETUP-Windows.bat to set it up and START-HERE-Windows.bat to start it. On Linux, run bash SETUP.command, then bash START-HERE.command. A kit on an exFAT drive starts on a Mac, Windows or Linux, whichever one set it up. Without a strong graphics card the AI is slower. Windows and Linux are newer to the kit, so they're tested less than the Mac.

How do I update it?

Whenever you like, while you're online, double-click SETUP again. There's no schedule. It gets the newest version of the kit first, so models, books and maps added since arrive too, and the picks you made stay. Only what changed downloads, and your old files keep working until the new ones check out.

How do I remove it?

Double-click UNINSTALL in the kit folder. Once you type delete, it removes the kit and everything it downloaded, and on a drive it leaves everything else alone.

Is all this content legal to download?

Yes, for your own use. The kit downloads everything straight from its publishers: Kiwix packages openly licensed sites like Wikipedia, WikiMed and iFixit, and US government guides, and the AI models are open-weight models published for anyone to run. Each keeps its own license, and some, like iFixit's, forbid commercial use, so don't sell drives you've filled. Every source and its license.
